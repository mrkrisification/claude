# Serbia Telecom Market — Structural Baseline

**Last updated:** 2026-06-09
**Covers:** 2024–2026 (structural history)

---

## Market Structure

Three mobile network operators; no operational MVNOs (blocked by spectrum/access policy until BABTO-backed legislative push in 2025–2026).

| Operator | Owner | Mobile share (subs) | Revenue trend | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Telekom Srbija (mts) | State of Serbia (58.11%) + minority shareholders | ~41.4% (~3.28M) | Record EUR 2.3B consolidated revenue (FY2025) | Dominant fixed incumbent; IPTV/content leader; acquired United Group's Western Balkans assets |
| Yettel Serbia | e& PPF Telecom Group (majority from Oct 2024) | ~32.4% (~2.56M) | Stable; converged FMC operator from April 2026 | Absorbed SBB Serbia on April 1, 2026; umlaut "Best in Test" network quality award W39 2025–W10 2026 |
| A1 Serbia (formerly Vip) | A1 Group (Telekom Austria) | ~26.2% (~2.07M) | Growing via porting gains; launched fixed 2025 | Consistent MNP leader Q4 2025 and Q1 2026; smallest base but fastest-growing |

**Total mobile market:** ~7.91M lines (declining from population emigration); 2025 market value ~USD 1.3B.
**Economy context:** Serbia GDP growth ~3.7% (2025); inflation easing. Market contraction is structural (emigration), not economic.

## Competitive History (2024–2026)

**2024: Ownership change and M&A reshuffling**
- e& (Etisalat Group) acquired majority of Yettel Serbia from PPF Group for approximately EUR 2.15B (transaction closed October 2024). Yettel Serbia is now under e& PPF Telecom Group, sharing ownership structure with Yettel Hungary and Yettel Bulgaria.
- United Group sold SBB Serbia (cable/broadband) to e& PPF for EUR 825M (signed early 2025, announced late 2024). Simultaneously, United Group sold Net TV Plus sports rights and content assets to Telekom Srbija.
- A1 Serbia launched fixed broadband service in 2025, entering fixed market as a third competitor alongside Telekom Srbija (ADSL/FTTH dominant) and Yettel/SBB (cable).

**2025: 5G spectrum auction and SBB integration**
- RATEL conducted landmark 5G spectrum auction in November 2025 across six frequency bands (700 MHz, 800 MHz, 1800 MHz, 2100 MHz, 2600 MHz, 3.5 GHz). All three operators each paid approximately EUR 100M (~EUR 300M total raised).
- Commercial 5G launched simultaneously by all three operators in December 2025, covering Belgrade and major urban centres. Obligations: minimum 200 base stations by December 2026, 1,000 by 2027; mandatory coverage of 22 cities plus key transport corridors; Belgrade Expo 2027 site is a named obligation.
- Yettel Serbia led early 5G base station deployment with 1,265 stations; A1 Serbia leads 5G availability metrics; Telekom Srbija leads 4G coverage.
- Serbia's mobile market shrank below 8M lines for first time, driven by sustained emigration.

**2026: FMC consolidation and porting battle**
- SBB Serbia legally merged into Yettel Serbia on April 1, 2026 — Yettel becomes a fully converged fixed-mobile operator with ~2.5M cable broadband subscribers absorbed. No Phase II competition probe was required.
- A1 Serbia sustained porting momentum: net +21,487 in Q4 2025, net +17,133 in Q1 2026; Telekom Srbija was the largest net loser in Q1 2026 (−19,641).
- Telekom Srbija CEO projected EUR 300M net profit for 2026 based on Q1 momentum; FY2025 EBIT was EUR 1.3B on EUR 2.3B consolidated revenue.
- Pricing discipline maintained across all three operators: entry-level tariff increases only once since 2022; competition manifests via bundle construction (unlimited tiers, eSIM promotions, tourist plans) rather than headline price cuts.

## Infrastructure State

**5G:**
- All three operators licensed and commercially live since December 2025
- Yettel: leads base station count (1,265 as of early 2026); also holds umlaut best-in-test network quality certification
- A1 Serbia: leads 5G availability metrics; private 5G campus pilots near Kragujevac (automotive sector)
- Telekom Srbija (mts): largest financial firepower; IoT enterprise bundles
- RATEL obligations: 200 BTS by December 2026, 1,000 by 2027; 22-city and Expo 2027 coverage mandatory

**Fixed:**
- Telekom Srbija: dominant fixed incumbent with ADSL/VDSL and FTTH rollout; IPTV/content market leader
- Yettel/SBB (merged): ~2.5M cable broadband subscribers inherited post-April 2026 merger
- A1 Serbia: entered fixed market in 2025; third-place challenger in fixed
- Serbia EU accession candidate: EU Gigabit Infrastructure Act norms influence regulatory roadmap; FTTP targets tracked

## Regulatory History

**Key regulator:** RATEL (Regulatory Agency for Electronic Communications and Postal Services); competition authority: KZK.

**2024:** e& PPF acquisition of Yettel Serbia cleared by KZK without Phase II. e& PPF acquisition of SBB Serbia from United Group also cleared. No MVNO access obligations imposed; five ISPs forming an MVNO consortium (BABTO-backed) initiated legal/regulatory proceedings to obtain wholesale mobile access.

**2025:** RATEL 5G spectrum auction (November 2025) — EUR ~300M raised; six bands awarded simultaneously to all three MNOs. No new entrants in 5G spectrum. MVNO legislative proposals remain pending (BABTO supporting legal changes, no final regulation adopted).

**2026:** SBB/Yettel merger closed April 1, 2026 without Phase II scrutiny. No SMP wholesale access changes identified. RATEL deployment obligations now in force (200 BTS by December 2026).

## Key Tensions to Track

1. **Yettel FMC integration** — SBB subscriber integration into Yettel's systems and commercial offers; whether converged bundles successfully retain cable base against A1 and Telekom Srbija fixed
2. **A1 porting momentum** — Can A1 sustain Q1 2026 porting pace? If so, market share could approach 27–28% by year-end, forcing Yettel and Telekom into tariff responses
3. **Telekom Srbija porting losses** — Largest incumbent absorbing consistent net porting outflows; financial firepower supports defence, but premium pricing risk increases with A1's promotional aggression
4. **5G coverage race to December 2026** — RATEL 200-BTS obligation creates a hard deadline; operators with largest capex (Telekom Srbija) will hit it first and use coverage claims commercially
5. **MVNO access** — BABTO-led consortium seeking wholesale access; if RATEL legislates MVNO access, new entrants would compress MNO margins from below (no resolution before 2027 likely)
6. **Yettel network quality vs. A1 porting** — Yettel holds the independent network quality certificate but is losing porting battles; gap between perception and execution is the central A1 opportunity
