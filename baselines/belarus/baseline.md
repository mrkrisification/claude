# Belarus Telecom Market — Structural Baseline

**Last updated:** 2026-06-09
**Covers:** 2024–2026 (structural history)

**Important note:** Belarus is under comprehensive EU and US sanctions regimes. English-language financial reporting on Belarusian operators is sparse. Per-operator subscriber share data is not publicly available; revenue figures below are from aggregated/estimated sources and should be treated as indicative.

---

## Market Structure

Three-player market under tight state influence. The state holds majority control over MTS Belarus (via Beltelecom) and indirectly shapes A1 and life:) operations through presidential decrees and the beCloud single-network 5G infrastructure model.

| Operator | Owner | Mobile share | Revenue (est.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 Belarus | A1 Group (Telekom Austria / América Móvil) | ~41.6% (~4.96M subs) | EUR 449M (FY2024); ~EUR 440M (FY2025 est.) | Largest private telecom; 8% of A1 Group revenue, 10% of EBITDA; acquired AtlantTelecom + TeleSet fixed-line operators (2025) |
| MTS Belarus | Beltelecom (51%, state) / MTS Russia (49%) | ~46% (~5.7M subs) | Not consolidated/disclosed (JV equity-accounted by MTS Russia) | State majority JV; rated best network by BeIGIE 2025 (886/1,000); 1,100 5G base stations |
| life:) (BeST) | Turkcell (100%, since Dec 2022) | ~12% (~1.5M subs) | <4% of Turkcell Group revenue (consolidated with international ops) | First operator to fully launch 5G commercially; >1,000 5G base stations; Turkcell injected BYN 40.9M capital (Feb 2025) |

**Total mobile market:** USD 1.07B (2025), projected USD 1.29B by 2030 (CAGR 3.75%).
**Active connections:** 11.4M (127% of population — multi-SIM market); 96.1% broadband-capable.

## Competitive History (2024–2026)

**2024: A1 deepens, life:) consolidates under Turkcell**
- Turkcell took 100% ownership of life:) (BeST) in December 2022 — fully consolidated. life:) operates largely out of the Western press radar; no English-language financial data publicly available.
- A1 Belarus: 41.6% mobile subscriber share, contributing 8% / 10% to A1 Group revenues / EBITDA. Financially material for A1 Group — a key reason no exit has been executed despite investor pressure.
- Market pricing: generally low by regional standards due to competitive pressure and regulatory constraints.

**2025: State controls tighten, 5G via presidential decree, A1 acquires fixed assets**
- April 2025: Presidential decree mandated basic social tariffs for all three operators, capping free-market pricing at floor level and restricting competitive tariff restructuring below the mandated minimum.
- April 2025: Presidential decree authorized a single national 5G investment project via beCloud (Belarusian Cloud Technologies LLC) — the state-owned wholesale infrastructure operator. No competitive spectrum auction; all three operators access 5G as tenants on beCloud's network.
- A1 Belarus acquired AtlantTelecom and its subsidiary TeleSet (Belarusian fixed-line operators, previously held by ZUBR Capital and the EBRD) in 2025, merging them with its mobile unit to become a full fixed-mobile convergent operator. This is an expansion, not an exit signal.
- Pricing: ITU mobile basket (140 min + 5 GB) fell ~27% YoY to USD 5.20 — the sharpest affordability improvement in the market in years, partly reflecting currency dynamics and competitive pressure above the social tariff floor.
- MTS Belarus: youth tariff (May 2025) for under-24 — unlimited internet + reduced fees; Russia-Belarus roaming agreement (March 2025) offering harmonized rates (150 min + 5 GB).
- life:) Belarus expanded 5G and LTE networks; implemented HD Voice.
- EU progressively tightened sanctions: 16th package (February 2025), 18th package (July 2025, adds SPFS transaction ban), new hybrid-activities criterion (December 2025).

**2026: beCloud takes 5G operational responsibility, EU 20th sanctions package**
- January 2026: beCloud formally assumed operational responsibility for 5G infrastructure; life:) leads with >1,000 base stations; MTS Belarus has 1,100, surpassing life:).
- April 2026: EU adopted 20th sanctions package (alongside Russia measures) with parallel Belarus amendments.
- A1 Belarus: Drive Unlimited tariff with aggressive MNP promotion (BYN 8.90/month first year for porters vs. BYN 39.90 standard); convergent #VsevDom bundle (BYN 29/month, 2 SIMs).
- MTS Belarus: Bezlimitishe (BYN 26.75/month unlimited); Grand Unlimited Pro convergent bundle with Beltelecom; Beltelecom/MTS convergent package expansion.
- US sanctions diverged: since September 2025, OFAC has been progressively relaxing Belarus sanctions in exchange for political prisoner releases (GL 14 authorizing Belinvestbank transactions issued March 2026); EU and US tracks now diverge materially.

## Infrastructure State

**5G:**
- Deployment model: state-directed via beCloud wholesale shared network — no competitive spectrum auctions
- Presidential decree (April 2025) authorized the phased national rollout:
  - Stage 1 (2025–2027): 70% Minsk coverage
  - Stage 2 (by 2028): 70% all regional centers
  - Stage 3 (by 2030): 99% Minsk + regional centers + cities >50k
  - Stage 4 (by 2034): 99% national coverage
- Current status (mid-2026): Live in Minsk, Brest, Vitsyebsk, Mahiliou, Homiel; Grodno pending equipment tuning
- MTS Belarus: 1,100 5G base stations; life:): >1,000; A1 Belarus status not confirmed publicly

**Fixed:**
- A1 Belarus now a full FMC operator following AtlantTelecom/TeleSet acquisition (2025)
- MTS Belarus: partnership with Beltelecom (state fixed incumbent) for convergent bundle distribution
- Beltelecom remains the dominant fixed incumbent

## Regulatory History

**Key bodies:** Ministry of Communications and Informatization (MinCom/MPTC) for licensing/spectrum; BeIGIE for network quality assessments; Operations and Analysis Center for cybersecurity.

**2022:** Turkcell completes 100% acquisition of life:) (BeST).

**2024:** No major regulatory changes. A1 continues to hold Belarus despite investor pressure.

**2025:** Presidential decree on social tariffs (April). Presidential decree on single 5G investment project via beCloud (April). EU sanctions packages 16th (February) and 18th (July). EU broadens Belarus sanctions criterion (December).

**2026:** beCloud formally takes 5G operational responsibility (January). EU 20th sanctions package (April). US OFAC relaxes Belarus sanctions (ongoing from September 2025). MTS Belarus named best network 2025 by BeIGIE.

**Note on sanctions:** EU sanctions (Regulation EC 265/2006 and amendments) explicitly prohibit supplying internet and telephone communications to Belarus, raising compliance risk for A1 Group specifically as an EU-headquartered company. A1 Group's legal basis for continuing to operate relies on carve-outs and has not been publicly challenged by the EC.

## Key Tensions to Track

1. **A1 Belarus divestiture risk** — A1 Group has instead deepened (acquired AtlantTelecom/TeleSet); but EU sanctions are progressively tightening while US sanctions ease; any EU enforcement action against A1 Group's Belarus operations would force a sale process
2. **EU vs. US sanctions divergence** — As OFAC eases, US investors/counterparties face fewer restrictions; European operators (A1) remain under EU sanctions; creates an asymmetric competitive environment
3. **beCloud 5G rollout pace** — State-directed timeline; Minsk Stage 1 progress by end-2027 will determine whether 5G becomes a meaningful commercial differentiator in the period
4. **Social tariff floor impact** — Presidential decree caps downside pricing; competition plays out above the floor through unlimited bundles and convergent offers; any loosening of the floor would trigger a repricing battle
5. **life:) under Turkcell** — Smallest operator, fully Turkcell-owned; no English-language commercial strategy data; potential upside from Turkcell's enterprise digital services portfolio if applied to Belarus
